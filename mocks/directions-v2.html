Direction 4

Friendly Native

The Amie/Family/Notion zone. Soft white surfaces, generous shadows, highlighter-marker emphasis, friendly squircle iconography. Warmth and precision in the same room. Not a serif in sight.

What's distinctive

Highlighter-marker emphasis on a single phrase per surface (Amie's signature move). Soft drop-shadows on cards, never hard borders. Squircle icons with personality. Cobalt CTAs that feel like Apple links, not Bootstrap buttons.

Risk

Hardest of the three to differentiate from the dozen other AI-startup sites using a similar move. We'd need the highlighter-marker plus the squircle "P" to do real work.

Direction 5

Architectural

The Samara zone. Massive monumental sans, real photography, black hero band, orange and cobalt as accents. Confident grown-up, not boutique. Treats the pool like the part of your home it actually is.

References: Samara · Aesop's app · MUJI · Cooper Hewitt · Watershed

What's distinctive

The type IS the design. Massive numbers and headlines treated like architectural signage. Black hero bands at the top of every page (like Samara), real photography of California backyards in the body. Orange and cobalt sparingly as functional accents, never decorative.

Risk

Lives or dies on photography. Without real, well-shot California-pool photography, this brand reads as a generic Vercel template. Photo budget is non-negotiable.

Direction 6

Cinematic Mood

The Superpower zone. Dark cinematic surfaces, golden-hour gradients, italic display serif (Instrument Serif) over photography, almost no chrome on the homepage. The data UI lives in a separate inner-app surface that inherits the warmth without the moodiness.

References: Superpower · Levels · Hims (recent rebrand) · The Fasting Method · Equinox

What's distinctive

Marketing surfaces lead with mood and photography, near-zero UI chrome. The italic Instrument Serif (the warmer Fraunces alternative most of the AI startups DON'T use yet) carries the headline. Inner-app screens inherit the dark + golden-hour palette but go quiet so data can breathe.

Risk

Two-mode brand: marketing site needs to feel cinematic AND product UI needs to be readable. Most companies fail at one or the other. Photography requirement is even higher than Architectural.

Six directions on the table now

Original three (Pool Club / Field Notebook / Operator) plus these three new ones (Friendly Native / Architectural / Cinematic Mood). Tell me which two or three to push deeper, or which one to apply to a real product screen next.

  • If you want warmth + product polish: Friendly Native is the safest path. Closest to "this app feels good to use every day." Risk: looks like every other 2026 AI startup.
  • If you want to feel grown-up and architectural: Architectural. Closest to the Samara-aesthetic. Best fit if PoolGo positions as "the home for the pool that came with your house."
  • If you want to feel destination-grade: Cinematic Mood. Closest to a category-defining lifestyle brand. Highest production-cost direction, biggest payoff if the photography is real.
  • From the original three: Field Notebook still holds up as the most distinctive. Operator still holds up as the boldest. Pool Club is the one I'd retire in favor of Architectural.
